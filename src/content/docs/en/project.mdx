## Born on a stage

Topos was designed at [Douze Dix-Huit](https://douzedixhuit.ch), by its technical stage manager, for one precise need: knowing in real time where the performers are, so that light and sound can follow them. Existing solutions are closed, expensive, or built for venues that have nothing in common with a neighbourhood theatre.

So we built our own, with off-the-shelf UWB modules, printed cases and Python. And since it works, we're sharing it.

## A spatial translator

Topos is neither a rendering engine nor a controller. It measures radio distances between tags and anchors, derives positions, and redistributes them in the protocols the show already uses. What you do with them — follow a performer with a moving head, spatialise a voice, fire a cue — is the business of your tools.

That is what keeps it small, and that is what keeps it reliable.

## The .red family

Topos is one of three tools sharing a single idea: linking the **real**, the **environment** it exists in, and its representation as **data**.

  <div><b>skena<i>.</i></b><span>represents — documenting and understanding a space</span></div>

Topos observes, Skena represents, Soma acts. Each works on its own; together they form a chain. Soma is a client of Topos, never the other way round.

## Where we stand

Topos is **beta** software, used in production on real shows, under active development. 2D and 3D tracking, PSN, OSC, ADM-OSC and Art-Net outputs, venue calibration, moving-head tracking and cues all work. What remains is listed in the repository; a public roadmap is coming to this site.
